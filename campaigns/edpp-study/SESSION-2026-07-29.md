## 1. What the session set out to do, and where it ended

**Starting question.** How do we obtain the *experimental* capacity ceiling of a
disaggregated fleet, as opposed to the analytical one, so that "policy X reaches
Y% of capacity" becomes a measured statement.

**Where it ended.** Four things, in descending order of confidence.

1. The analytical ceiling the study had been using, `pd_best`, was **not a
   ceiling**. It is corrected, the correct one is derived, and the correct one is
   now measured on two workloads.
2. The paper's capacity section was rebuilt on the corrected bound, the Slater
   condition was connected to it, and a rendering bug affecting fourteen equations
   was fixed. Committed and pushed to Overleaf.
3. The goodput comparison that justified the routing rule was **measuring
   nothing**, because every baseline reading was taken at a load where the
   baseline was saturated by construction. Rebuilt.
4. On the rebuilt comparison, **a well-chosen static plan beats the adaptive rule
   in every regime measured**, homogeneous and heterogeneous. The reason is
   diagnosed precisely.

**And one limitation discovered at the very end that scopes all of item 4**, in
§11 below. Every workload used today had a *constant* input length and no prefix
reuse, which is exactly the condition under which a fixed plan cannot be beaten by
per-request adaptivity. The negative results are real but they were measured in a
setting that structurally cannot favour the rule.

## 3. `pd_best` was not a ceiling

**The defect.** `campaigns/edpp-study/gamma_cap.py` computed `pd_best` by giving
each mixed instance a *time share* θ for prefill and `1−θ` for decode, and pricing
the two shares separately at `t_P` and `t_D`. That counts the per-iteration cost α
**once for each stage**. A mixed instance carrying a prefill chunk in the same
iteration as its decode batch pays α **once for both**. So `pd_best` over-counted
iterations for any policy that collocates, under-stated the fleet, and was not an
upper bound on 1P2D policies.

`infocom/main.tex` already conceded this at line 501 and left the shortfall
unmeasured.

**The correction.** On a 1P2D fleet a request has exactly three placements, and
one is dominated:

| placement | mixed-pool time consumed |
|---|---|
| (a) prefill on instance_0, decode on a mixed instance | `t_D` |
| (b) prefill and decode on the same mixed instance | `t_coll` |
| (c) prefill on mixed *i*, decode on mixed *j*≠*i* | `t_P + t_D` |

(c) is dominated because `t_coll < t_P + t_D` by exactly one α, and only the pool
total appears in the capacity sum. **This dominance argument assumes the mixed
instances are identical and is false on heterogeneous hardware** — see §10.

So one number describes the family: **φ, the share of arrivals that
disaggregate.** With `|M|` mixed instances,

```
rho_0     = lambda * phi * t_P                                    (prefill instance)
rho_mixed = (lambda/|M|) * [ phi*t_D + (1-phi)*t_coll ]           (each mixed instance)

C(phi) = min( 1/(phi*t_P) , |M| / (phi*t_D + (1-phi)*t_coll) )
```

Both endpoints recover a real policy, **which is what makes the family
measurable**: φ=1 is `always`, φ=0 is `never`.

**How run.** `python3 campaigns/edpp-study/mix_cap.py` from the repo root. Batch
cap 256, chunk budget 2048, packed prefill, output distribution Monte-Carloed
rather than evaluated at the mean because the decode cost is quadratic in output
length and the two differ by about 2%.

| cell | `pd_best` (old) | **C_mix (correct)** | excess | **φ\*** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| decode | 40.38 | 40.38 | 0% | 1.000 |
| mixed | 58.38 | **70.13** | **+20%** | 0.485 |
| prefill_lean | 20.01 | **21.49** | **+7%** | 0.387 |
| prefill_bound | 11.78 | **12.33** | **+5%** | 0.337 |

All req/s for the whole three-instance fleet.

**An identity fell out**, verified to four decimals on all four cells:

```
phi* = C_always / C_mix(phi*)
```

where `C_always = min(1/t_P, sum_M 1/t_D)`. It holds by derivation wherever the
lone prefill instance binds under full separation (mixed, prefill_lean,
prefill_bound) and trivially on decode, where φ\* sits at the boundary. In words:
*the share you should disaggregate equals the share of your own ceiling that
disaggregating everything reaches.*

**Notation warning.** θ (retired) was a *time* share; φ is a *request* share. They
move in opposite directions as prompts lengthen, so the old and new numbers look
contradictory unless you know which is which:

| cell | θ\* (retired) | φ\* |
|---|---|---|
| decode | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| mixed | 0.36 | 0.485 |
| prefill_lean | 0.703 | 0.387 |
| prefill_bound | 0.920 | 0.337 |

Both rows for decode describe the same policy, `always`.

## 4. Measuring the ceiling

**Method and why.** A capacity bound quantifies over all assignments and no router
realizes one we choose, so the assignment has to be held from outside the routing
rule. `--pd-plan <csv>` (`cmd/root.go:1345`) forces a per-request
`(decode_instance, prefill_instance)` plan and overrides `--pd-decider`. A plan
holding φ fixed realizes one point of `C(φ)`; sweeping φ traces the curve and its
peak is the ceiling. `prefill_instance = local` means no disaggregation at all
(`sim/fixed_plan_decider.go:16`), which is what lets a plan express collocation.

Plans are generated by `make_pd_plan.py` with Bresenham interleaving rather than
sampling, *because* we are measuring a long-run property and any extra variance is
noise. Realized φ is exact to four decimals.

**How run.** `campaigns/edpp-study/repro_mix_ceiling.sh`. Every point is offered
**1.10–1.15× its own predicted capacity**, not one large rate for all φ. *Why:*
what triggers shedding is the backlog, not the rate, so a single large rate sheds
requests at exactly the φ values whose capacity is lowest — and a run that sheds
is not a capacity measurement. N = 4600 requests (2600 in the peak re-run).

**Self-check.** A plan at φ=1 must reproduce `--pd-decider always` and φ=0 must
reproduce `never`. Measured agreement: **0.02%**. Without that, no interior point
would be trustworthy.

**Results** (seed 42, all runs gated clean):

| | prefill_lean (8192/64) | prefill_bound (16000/16) |
|---|---|---|
| **measured ceiling** | **19.75** req/s at φ=0.39 | **11.64** req/s at φ=0.34 |
| predicted φ\* | 0.387 | 0.337 |
| `always` (φ=1) | 8.2765 → 0.42 of ceiling | 4.1454 → 0.36 |
| `never@1P2D` (φ=0) | 13.5430 → 0.69 | 7.9062 → 0.68 |

The peak sits where the model says on two workloads differing 2× in prompt length
and 4× in generation length. `never@3M` predicted/measured ratio 3/2 recovered
independently.

**A second finding: model accuracy splits by which instance binds.**

| binding side | measured / predicted | points |
|---|---|---|
| lone prefill instance | 0.989 – 0.999 | 10 |
| collocated mixed pool | 0.939 – 0.954 | 13 |

This retires a standing puzzle — why `always` always matched prediction and
`never` always came in ~9% low. It was never noise. The residual belongs to
collocated serving alone.

**Achieved batch occupancy**, exported for this (new `mean_running_batch` field):
mixed instances run 3–24 against the 256 cap, and the dedicated prefill instance
runs **1.10**. The 1.10 *confirms* the paper's own claim that a prompt filling a
chunk leaves its iteration carrying nothing else. Substituting the measured mean
into the decode term overshoots the residual roughly threefold, because the mean
runs over all iterations including the prefill-chunk steps already accounted for
separately — so the residual has a location and not yet an account.

## 5. Three protocol traps found

1. **Deep overload sheds requests silently.** `dropped_unservable` fired on a run
   that would otherwise have placed the optimum at the wrong φ (it read 10.84
   instead of 11.16 at φ=0.31, enough to make the curve look like it peaked one
   grid point early). Older harnesses do not gate on it, though the runs on record
   are clean.
2. **`sim.CalculateMean` divides by 1000.** It folds a ticks-to-milliseconds
   conversion into a function named only "mean". Using it for a batch count would
   have reported 0.256 instead of 256. `meanInt` in `sim/metrics.go` exists because
   of this.
3. **The TraceV2 export's `status` field contradicts the metrics.** A run reporting
   `completed_requests: 1000, still_queued: 0, still_running: 0` exported 387 rows
   marked `incomplete` with zero timestamps. Not chased; worth an issue.

Also: `responses_per_sec` divides completed requests by a duration that includes
the post-arrival drain. On the decode archetype a request lives ~13 s, so a short
arrival window makes a perfectly healthy run look saturated. The gate now
compares against a drain-corrected expectation `N/(window + mean_E2E)`.

## 7. The goodput comparison was a tautology

**The defect.** Every `always` goodput on record read 0.000 — and every one was
measured *above* `always`'s own capacity (8.28 req/s on prefill_lean, 4.15 on
prefill_bound), where a saturated policy misses every deadline by construction.
Those readings restated the capacity result rather than measuring routing.

**The fix.** `campaigns/edpp-study/repro_goodput_ladder.sh`. Three rates per cell
**below** `always`'s ceiling, rates expressed as fractions of the *measured*
ceiling, three arms, no `never` in any form. The third arm is `plan@φ*`, a forced
plan at the throughput-optimal share, *because* that is the static reference an
adaptive rule has to beat to earn its complexity. 300 s of arrivals, seeds 42 and
7, 60 runs, all gated clean.

| cell | rate | ×ceiling | `always` | `dpvar` | `plan@φ*` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill_lean | 4 | 0.20 | 0.493 ±0.018 | 0.804 ±0.037 | **0.867** ±0.020 |
| prefill_lean | 6 | 0.30 | 0.253 ±0.011 | 0.726 ±0.034 | **0.803** ±0.014 |
| prefill_lean | 8 | 0.41 | 0.033 ±0.061 | 0.609 ±0.048 | **0.707** ±0.032 |
| prefill_bound | 2 | 0.17 | 0.508 ±0.063 | 0.801 ±0.022 | **0.876** ±0.022 |
| prefill_bound | 3 | 0.26 | 0.230 ±0.062 | 0.708 ±0.007 | **0.807** ±0.040 |
| prefill_bound | 4 | 0.34 | 0.031 ±0.053 | 0.646 ±0.029 | **0.718** ±0.038 |

**Good news.** At prefill_lean rate 4 all three arms serve 4.03 req/s — identical

`always` sits at 48% of its own capacity. Replicated on prefill_bound.

**`always` fails purely on first-token time.** Its ITL attainment is *exactly*

**Bad news.** `plan@φ*` beats dpVaR on all ten rate points by 0.063–0.117 against
seed spreads of 0.007–0.083.

## 8. Why the fixed plan wins — diagnosed

**Method.** `cmd/root.go:2535` prints `Disaggregated Requests: N` on every run, so
the *realized* share is N over injected. The ladder discarded stdout, hence a
separate pass, `repro_realized_share.sh`, reusing the ladder's own specs so the
arrival traces are byte-identical. `always` (must read 1.0000) and the plan (must
read φ\*) are controls; both passed exactly.

| cell | rate | **dpVaR realized φ** | goodput-optimal φ | direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill_lean | 4 | **0.0850** | 0.34 | far too low |
| prefill_lean | 6 | 0.2189 | 0.34 | too low |
| prefill_lean | 8 | 0.3533 | 0.34 | about right |
| prefill_bound | 2 | **0.0367** | 0.30 | far too low |
| prefill_bound | 4 | 0.2083 | 0.50 | too low |
| decode | 12 | **0.8169** | 0.00 | **far too high** |
| decode | 24 | 0.8079 | 0.00 | far too high |
| hetero (ψ, see §10) | 120 | **0.109** | 0.30 | too low |
| hetero (ψ) | 180 | 0.107 | 0.50 | far too low, and did not move |

**One defect, three appearances.** The share the rule selects does not track the
goodput optimum in any regime, and does not respond to load. It splits far too
rarely where prompts are long and far too often where they are short, which is the
*inverse* of the correct ordering.

**Mechanism, consistent throughout.** Disaggregating adds a KV transfer to the
first-token path (`eq:ttft-disagg`) that is large and **certain**. The queueing it
avoids is **neither**, because the roll-forward reads the current batch and cannot
see the standing queue that collocating everything is about to build. The rule pays
a visible cost to avoid an invisible one. On the decode cell the sign flips: a
256-token prompt makes the prefill instance look idle and the transfer look cheap,
so the estimate prefers to split, and it cannot see that the split request then
waits for admission into a 256-deep decode batch while a collocated chunk rides
the next iteration. Same snapshot blindness, opposite sign. This is the property
`infocom/main.tex` Model Validation already documents, surfacing as a routing bias
rather than an estimator error.

## 10. Heterogeneous hardware

**Why this was the remaining case.** `infocom/main.tex:173` already scopes the
paper by saying operators disaggregate for reasons beyond throughput, including
running different accelerator generations in the two pools. A fixed *share* cannot
express "never decode on the slow box", so this looked like the place where
adaptivity does something a constant structurally cannot.

**The trap, and why the experiment is built the way it is.** `--pd-plan` names the
**decode instance** per request, not just a share, so a static plan *can* steer
decode away from a slow accelerator. Comparing the rule only against shares would
have been rigged in its favour. The static family here is therefore
two-dimensional, and `make_pd_plan.py` gained a `--psi` knob: **ψ is the share of
decodes sent to the slow instance.** ψ=0 quarantines it, ψ=0.50 is an even split.
Every plan holds φ=1 so prefill is fixed on `instance_0` and the decode assignment
is the only knob.

**This also exposes a limitation in the §III now committed.** The dominance
argument that retired placement (c) — prefill on one mixed instance, decode on
another — assumed *identical* mixed instances. On heterogeneous hardware it is
false, and that arrangement is precisely the quarantine. §III needs a sentence
scoping the claim to the homogeneous case.

**Fleet.** `campaigns/edpp-study/out/hetero_real_cmp/bundle.yaml`: fast pool H100
(1979 TFLOPS, 3.35 TB/s, 8 GPUs → 2 instances at TP4), slow pool A100 (**624
TFLOPS, 2.039 TB/s** — real hardware specs, 4 GPUs → 1 instance).
`coeffs-llama70b-a100real-tp4.json` gives α_D = **25.56 ms** against the H100's
16.61, a factor of 1.54. *Why not the earlier bundle:* it used a fabricated 0.7
TB/s giving α_D = 69.31 ms, a factor of 4.17, which Vishakha ruled out. Every
heterogeneous number from before 2026-07-29 is void, and `gamma_cap.py:105` still
loads that crippled file.

**Instance mapping measured, not assumed** — a plan names instances explicitly and
getting it backwards would invert the experiment. Probe run at rate 60, reading
per-instance ITL:

| instance | mean ITL | identity |
|---|---|---|
| instance_0 | — | fast H100, prefill-only |
| instance_1 | 17.48 ms | fast H100, mixed |

**The mechanism.** The derived ITL target is **25.04 ms** and the slow instance's
per-iteration floor is **25.56 ms**, so it cannot meet the inter-token target on a
single request at any batch size. Every request it decodes misses. This is a
property of the hardware against the SLO, not congestion, so no headroom removes
it. **The margin is 2%. It survives with honest coefficients and it is a knife
edge** — the target is the reference fleet's mean ITL, so a different fleet mix
moves it and could erase the effect.

| ψ=0 | 0.542 |

**Plain `always` has the lowest worst-case regret of anything tested and beats
dpVaR by 2.8×.**

**What supports the premise.** The optimal ψ **does move with load** — 0.30 at
rate 120, 0.50 at rate 180. ψ=0.30 scores 0.587 at the lower rate and 0.009 at the
higher one. So no fixed ψ is optimal everywhere, exactly as the paper argues. But
"no constant is optimal" and "our rule beats a constant" are different statements,
and only the first holds.

**What dpVaR does right, and wrong.** It quarantines *without being told which
instance is slow*, reading only per-instance coefficients — ITL 0.89 where the even
split gets 0.50. The capability is real. But its `slow_dec` is 0.109 and 0.107 at
the two rates, essentially **load-invariant**, while the optimum moves 0.30 → 0.50.
It over-quarantines, concentrating load on the fast instance, and its TTFT collapses
to 0.359 and then 0.034.

## 11. The limitation that scopes every negative result above

Discovered at the end of the session, on Vishakha's prompting about workload
choice. **It is the most important caveat in this document.**

Verified on the actual spec files used:

```
input_distribution: {type: constant, params: {value: 8192}}   # and 16000, 2048, 256
prefix_length: 0
```

**Every workload used today had a constant input length and no prefix reuse.** The
only per-request variation was output length, which the router may not read
(INV-9, the oracle knowledge boundary).

So **at routing time every request was indistinguishable from every other.** There
was nothing to condition a per-request decision on. In that setting a fixed plan is
optimal *by construction*, because the optimal policy is a single distribution over
placements and a fixed plan realizes exactly that. The adaptive rule's only
possible lever was reacting to instantaneous state, which is a much weaker one.

**This does not make the negative results wrong.** They are correctly measured, the
diagnosis of the rule's share selection stands, and a reviewer would be right to
run exactly these experiments. But it does mean **the evaluation never gave
per-request adaptivity anything to be adaptive about.**

Two further consequences:

- `gamma_cap.py` and `mix_cap.py` have **no cached-prefix term**. `t_P` is computed
  on the full prompt. With prefix reuse the prefill work is only the uncached
  suffix (`infocom/main.tex` `eq:wp` already carries the cached count), so the
  capacity model needs extending before it can be trusted on a prefix-heavy
  workload.
- A workload with a **bimodal** input distribution is the case where a fixed share
  should genuinely fail. A static plan assigns by position in the stream; an
  adaptive rule can assign by prompt length. That is a lever a constant does not
  have, and it has never been tested.

Vishakha's direction: stop using the hand-rolled synthetic cells and test

| spec | character | key properties |
|---|---|---|
| `inference-perf-batch-synthetic-data-generation.yaml` | decode-bound, catalog `prefill_decode_ratio: 0.125` | 2000-token shared prefix; input lognormal mean 500 **σ large, tail to 15000**; output gaussian mean 4000, to 8000 |

**Why these are better than what we used.** They are public and citable, which
answers "synthetic and cherry-picked". They carry prefix reuse, so prefix-aware
routing becomes measurable. Their input distributions are heavy-tailed and bimodal
rather than constant, which is exactly the condition §11 says was missing. And the
shared-prefix spec has *load stages*, so the load dependence we had to construct by
hand comes built in.

**What has to be built or fixed before they can be used.**

1. **A cached-prefix term in the capacity model.** `t_P` must use the uncached
   suffix. Without it, `C(φ)` will over-state prefill cost on a workload with a
   2000-token shared prefix and every ceiling will be wrong.
2. **Prefix-aware routing enabled.** These runs used `queue-depth:1` only. The
   specs' `prefix_group`/`prefix_length` need a `precise-prefix-cache` scorer for
   the cache to matter.
3. **Capacity and targets re-derived per workload.** Rates today were fractions of
   measured ceilings that do not exist for these specs yet.
4. **KV pressure checked.** The RAG doc-read client reaches 80000-token inputs. The
   gates already catch `dropped_unservable`, and it should be expected here.
5. **Repoint `gamma_cap.py:105`** off the crippled A100.

## 13. What survives, and what is withdrawn

**Survives, measured and validated repeatedly.**

- The capacity model. `always` predicted to 0.999 across a fourfold span of offered
  load; the φ curve peaks where predicted on two workloads; the identity
  φ\*=C_always/C_mix exact to four decimals on four cells; the 3/2 instance-count
  ratio recovered from two independent measurements; monotone capacity in ψ on
  heterogeneous hardware. **Six independent experiments, no contradiction.**
- The two-regime accuracy split, which explains a previously unexplained residual.
- The ITL-infeasibility mechanism on heterogeneous hardware, with the slow
  instance's ITL attainment coming out as exactly `1 − ψ` at every setting.
- dpVaR beats `always` decisively at matched throughput on the prefill cells
  (1.6×), and it does perform the heterogeneous quarantine unprompted.
- The corrected paper §III, and the Slater-to-capacity bridge.

**Withdrawn or overturned.**

- `pd_best` as a ceiling, and every fraction-of-ceiling number computed against it.
- Every pre-2026-07-28 goodput number (concurrency 16 froze ITL).
- Every pre-2026-07-29 heterogeneous number (fabricated A100 bandwidth).
- Every `always` goodput of 0.000 as evidence about routing.
- The claim that the rule under-disaggregates — too narrow; it mis-selects in both
  directions.
- My own prediction that the bias would help on the decode cell. It over-splits
  there (0.82 against an optimum of 0.00).

**Unresolved.**

- Whether the rule's share selection can be fixed. The cause is identified and
  specific, and a fix is a change to the rule's core needing its own validation.
- Whether per-request adaptivity wins on a workload with per-request signal. §11
  says this has never been tested, and §12 is how to test it.
